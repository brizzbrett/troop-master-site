## Home

**Hero** (image: the wordmark over `hero.png`)

> **Build the camp by day. Hold it by night.**
>
> A survival game about a troop of Wilderness Scouts camped somewhere they should not be.
>
> [Signup form] · *PC · In development*

**The loop** (image: `camp-day.png` / `camp-night.png` drag slider, caption "The same camp at 1 PM and 11 PM.")

> ### Two games, one camp
> By day it plays as a colony sim. Your scouts gather wood and water, raise a camp, take orders and get on with their own work when you are not watching.
>
> By night the animals that live in the caves come for the camp all at once, and the same game becomes a tower defense.
>
> Dawn settles the bill for both: the upkeep, the damage, and who is still standing.

**The scouts** (images: `scout-card.png`, the four mood faces)

> ### The scouts are people, not units
> Every scout has a name, a face they keep for the whole run, a mood that answers to how much attention you pay them, and one or two traits you can't change.
>
> Rank is earned, never assigned. There is no class menu: the archer is the scout who kept making clean shots. Six field badges and thirty-eight perks take a Recruit all the way to Master Cadet.
>
> ### Nobody dies
> A scout who goes down is knocked out and goes home on the noon bus. Unless you built a First Aid Tent and saved the herbs to keep them. A night can still cost you someone, and the morning report remembers who.

**The four maps** (four cards: emblem + biome screenshot)

> ### Four wildernesses, each harder than the last
> - **Forest.** The gentle start. Ponds, berry bushes, plenty of stone, and fireflies after dark. Squirrels, foxes and bears.
> - **Jungle.** Hidden snares and sinkholes, vines that grab, poison, and flash floods. Spiders, snakes and gorillas.
> - **Desert.** No water but what you tap from cacti. Sandstorms, dust devils, and mirages that lure your workers away. Scorpions, jackals and warthogs.
> - **Tundra.** Nights cold enough to hurt anyone left outside, blizzards, thin ice, and the aurora overhead. Lemmings, wolves and yetis.
>
> Every map is generated fresh for each run.

**The alphas** (strip of `alpha-*` cut-outs: the 12 rotation alphas only)

> ### Every tenth night, something bigger
> A named boss walks out of the dark: the Honey Bandit, the Pack Leader, the Silverback, the Tusker and eight more. Each one telegraphs its attacks on the ground, and each one enrages when it's nearly beaten. Win and you keep a prize: a heart, a trophy, or its cub as your camp's mascot.
>
> Some of them aren't in the rotation. You'll have to find those yourself.

**Living world** (images: `aurora-night.png`, `mirage-tree.png`, `raid-breach.png`)

> ### The wilderness doesn't wait for you
> Animals forage, steal from your stores and drag the loot back to their dens. Weather rolls through: rain, storms, sandstorms, blizzards. A storyteller watches how comfortable your camp has gotten and sends a festival, a wildfire or a herd through accordingly. By Day 30 the night brings hundreds of animals at once.

## The Game (`/game/`)

Intro: *Everything Troop Master is, in more detail than the front page.*

| Section | Text | Image |
|---|---|---|
| The day | Dawn is 5 AM and nightfall is 7 PM, and the day gets most of the clock because it is where the game is played. Right-click a tree, a pond or a stone with a scout selected and they go, and keep going until the job is done. Order buildings from the HQ board, set each patrol's standing jobs, and raid an animal den for its hoard — if three scouts can hold the line while they do. | `den-odds.png` |
| Attention is a resource | A scout left on auto-work with no orders slowly gets neglected, and it shows. Two ignored days and they're Glum; further down and they stop volunteering for anything. Giving a real order pays that off. The day job is not just logistics. It is looking at your scouts. | `scout-card.png` |
| The night | At 7 PM the caves start releasing animals in synchronized waves. A third of them hunt your scouts; the rest go for the walls, the towers and the Storage. Towers fire on their own or with a scout posted on them. Walls go up a quarter of the ring at a time, and each quarter falls all at once. | `raid-wall.png` |
| The forecast | Before dusk the forecast tells you how many raiders are coming and from which side, grades the camp READY, SHAKY or OUTMATCHED, and offers tonight's plan: a few moves that lift the grade, which you tick and confirm. | `night-plan.png` |
| Dawn | Every building posts its upkeep to one account, and dawn pays what the stores can afford. What's left owed wears down the HQ. The morning report gives every scout a row: kills, damage, healing, repairs, the day's MVP, and who is out. | `morning-report.png` |
| The camp | The HQ grows through three levels, and each one opens new buildings: shelters, campfires, a First Aid Tent, refineries for planks, bricks and rations, walls and towers, and training grounds. Upgrading takes rare materials you have to win from dens and alphas. | `board-build.png` |
| The wilderness | Thirteen species, each with its own life by day. Foragers rob your Storage; heavies hunt scouts; birds mind their own business until you cut down their tree. From Day 5 elites start showing up, and they keep getting more common. | `dust-devil.png` |
| Weather and the storyteller | Each map rolls its own sky, and weather changes how the game plays: blizzards put out campfires, sandstorms slow everyone, fog shortens how far your towers can see, lightning starts wildfires. On top of that, a storyteller schedules incidents based on how comfortable you are. | `aurora-night.png` |
| Between runs | Runs end. What carries forward is popcorn, spent in the Popcorn Shop on 28 upgrades; the Evidence Board, collecting the papers you find out in the wild; 40 achievements; and, once your first alpha falls, the Pact: nine conditions to make the next run harder on purpose. | `evidence.png` |

**Short description** (one sentence, quotable)

> Troop Master is a survival game where a troop of teenage Wilderness Scouts builds a camp by day and holds it against the animals of the caves by night.

**Long description**

> Troop Master is a survival game about a troop of Wilderness Scouts camped somewhere they should not be. By day it plays as a colony sim: your scouts gather wood and water, raise a camp, take orders and get on with their own work when you are not watching. By night the animals that live in the caves around you come for the camp all at once, and the same game becomes a tower defense. Dawn settles the bill for both.
>
> The scouts are people, not units. Each has a name, a face and a mood that answers to how much attention you pay them, and earns rank by doing the work. Nobody dies: a scout who goes down is knocked out and sent home on the bus. But a night without them still costs the camp.
>
> Four maps, from a gentle Forest to a freezing Tundra, each generated fresh for every run. Night raids that scale to hundreds of animals at once. A boss every tenth night. And a rival troop whose paperwork keeps turning up in the wild.

**Features**
- A colony sim by day and a tower defense by night, in the same camp, on the same clock
- Named scouts with moods, traits, six field badges and 38 perks; rank is earned, never assigned
- Nobody dies: knocked-out scouts go home on the noon bus unless you saved a bed and the herbs to keep them
- Four maps with their own animals, weather, hazards and rules
- Night raids that grow to hundreds of animals, and a named alpha boss every tenth night
- A storyteller that reads how comfortable your camp is and sends trouble accordingly
- A story told through 53 painted documents left by Troop 13
- Between runs: the Popcorn Shop, 40 achievements, and the Pact for players who want it harder

**About the developer**

> Bretts Games is one person. Troop Master has been in development since 2017 and moved to Unity 6 in 2026.

## FAQ (`/faq/`)

| Q | A |
|---|---|
| When is it out? | There's no date yet. Join the email list and you'll hear first. |
| What platforms? | PC (Windows). Mouse and keyboard. |
| How much will it cost? | Not decided yet. |
| Is it on Steam? | Not yet. When the store page goes up, this site will link to it. |
| Can scouts die? | No. A scout who goes down is knocked out and goes home on the bus, unless you can afford to keep them. |
| How hard is it? | There's no difficulty setting; the game is balanced as one game. The Popcorn Shop makes later runs easier, the Pact makes them harder, and a gentler Peaceful days option turns the night raids off entirely. |
| How long is a run? | A good first run lasts around twenty in-game days, about three and a half hours. You can change how long the days run. |
| Is there a tutorial? | Yes: an optional guided first day that walks you through building the camp and your first raid. |
| Can I play it now? | Not publicly. There are friends-and-family playtest builds; the email list is the way to hear about wider tests. |
| Can I stream or make videos of it? | Yes, once you can play it. |
